# LinkedIn Profile Photo Brief

## Why It Matters

- Profiles with photos receive **21x more views** and **9x more connection requests**
- Your photo is the first thing people see in search results, comments, messages, and post feeds
- In the creative/art world, it signals you're a real professional, not a ghost account

## Technical Specs

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Minimum size | **400 x 400 px** (displays as a circle) |
| Maximum size | 7680 x 4320 px |
| Max file size | 8 MB |
| Format | JPG or PNG |
| Display | Circular crop on LinkedIn |

## What to Aim For

- **Framing:** Shoulders up, face filling ~60% of the circular frame
- **Expression:** Genuine, approachable, slight smile or neutral-confident. Not goofy, not stern.
- **Background:** Clean and non-distracting. Options:
  - Solid dark/neutral wall (safest)
  - Blurred studio/workspace (adds context)
  - In front of one of your installations (if it looks natural, not posed)
- **Lighting:** Natural light preferred (face a large window). Avoid harsh overhead fluorescents or direct flash.
- **Clothing:** Clean, dark or neutral top. No logos, no busy patterns. You're in the creative/art world -- you don't need a suit, but look intentional.
- **Recency:** Within the last 2-3 years. People should recognize you from your photo.

## What to AVOID

- Selfies (arm visible, distorted perspective)
- Cropped group photos (pixelated, awkward framing)
- Vacation/party photos
- Filters, heavy retouching, or black & white (looks outdated on LinkedIn)
- Sunglasses or hats obscuring your face
- Photos where you're too far away (face should be prominent in the circle)

### Option 2: DIY with a Colleague (~free, 15 min)

If budget or time is tight, this can produce a perfectly good result:

1. Find a large window with soft, even light (overcast days are best)
2. Stand 3-4 feet from a clean, neutral wall
3. Have a colleague use the rear camera of a recent smartphone in **Portrait Mode**
4. Frame from mid-chest up
5. Take 20-30 shots with slight variations in expression and angle
6. Pick the best one, crop to square, adjust brightness/contrast slightly

### Option 3: Contextual Shot at an Installation

If you have a high-quality photo of yourself taken at one of your installations (exhibition opening, press documentation), this can work well -- it adds context and signals your work. Requirements:
- Your face is clearly visible and well-lit
- The background is interesting but not distracting
- It doesn't look like a candid/surveillance shot
- Quality is high enough to crop to a clear 400x400+ headshot
